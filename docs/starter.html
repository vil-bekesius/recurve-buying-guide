Starter kit

Your first bow of your own. I recommend choosing each part yourself: a good CNC-machined riser that will stay with you for years, with cheap limbs and arrows that you replace as your draw weight goes up.

Buy ILF, not a bolt-on training bow. ILF (International Limb Fitting) limbs fit nearly every ILF riser, so you can keep the riser and change limbs as your draw weight goes up. One-piece and bolt-on training bows can't be upgraded this way.

Who this is for

  • You've finished a beginners course and shoot every week.
  • Your draw weight is still going up, so you want cheap limbs and arrows that you'll replace within a year.

Where to spend and where to save

  • Spend on the riser, sight, pressure button and stabilisers. You don't grow out of them, and they move with you to intermediate kit.
  • Save on limbs and arrows. Keep first limbs cheap: you'll replace limbs and arrows as your draw weight goes up.

The riser: buy once, cry once

Buy an intermediate-level riser now, rather than an entry riser you'll replace in a year or two. For the best quality for the price, I recommend CNC-machined risers from Mybo and WNS. See the intermediate risers for more choices.

Stabilisers: start with a long rod

A long rod is enough to begin with. Over time, you'll add an extender, a V-bar and two side rods. Choose a long rod from a range that also sells these parts, so they match when you complete the set.

Check the bow when you buy it

  • Look closely at the riser. Check both limb pockets and the riser body for anything that isn't symmetrical, such as one limb pocket sitting differently from the other.
  • Check the barebow weights. If the riser comes with barebow weights, make sure they fit as they should.
