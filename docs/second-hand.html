Buying second-hand

Second-hand kit can save a lot, especially limbs while your draw weight is still rising, but check every part first. Club members often sell their old kit, so ask at your club.

PartWhat to check
RiserHold it before you buy; grip fit matters. Most risers are strong and worn paint is cosmetic. Avoid a cast riser with an unknown history (a drop can crack it) and risers with custom paint jobs, which may have been oven-baked.
LimbsYou can't tell if limbs have been dry-fired (shot without an arrow). Reject limbs with splits or cracks along the edges. Crazed lacquer on old limbs is cosmetic, but pay less for it. Cheap used limbs suit you while your draw weight is changing.
ArrowsArrows are cut and spined for one archer, so used arrows rarely fit. Buy arrows new in a shop.
SightBuy budget sights new. A good used Shibuya, Sure-Loc or Axcel sight is often a good buy; check that the windage and elevation move smoothly with no play.
Stabilisers, stands, quiversParts with no mechanism are usually safe to buy used. Check rods for cracks and damaged threads.

Buying safely

  • Price: about half the new price is fair for good condition. Don't get carried away in online auctions.
  • Payment: check what buyer protection your payment method gives before you pay.
  • Set-up: set up the bow before you shoot it. If you are unsure, pay a shop to set it up and check it; most UK shops charge for technician time (see UK shops).
  • Second opinion: ask an experienced club member or coach to look at a part before you buy it.
