Intermediate kit

What to upgrade next, once you shoot regularly and enter club competitions. If you bought a good riser at the start, you keep it: most upgrades from here are small parts, plus new limbs and arrows as your draw weight goes up.

Who this is for

  • You shoot two or three times a week and enter club or county shoots.
  • You're working towards Archery GB classifications (Archer, Bowman and Master Bowman tiers).
  • Your draw weight is still going up, but more slowly than in your first year.

Where to spend: the same rule as for starter kit. Why →

Most archers add a clicker after three to six months of consistent shooting.

Stepping up in draw weight

  • Move up only when you're ready. Consider the next step in draw weight only when a full day of shooting leaves you with little or no fatigue.
  • Step up by about 2 lb. The best choice is new limbs that, fully wound out, are about 2 lb heavier than your old limbs fully wound in. This gives you room to grow without shocking your muscles with a big jump.
  • Ask about limb exchange. Some shops run limb exchange schemes for archers moving up in draw weight.

Limbs: cheap until your draw weight is stable

Keep buying budget limbs while your draw weight is still going up. Buy better limbs only once your draw weight has stayed the same for at least six months.

Arrows: cheap aluminium-carbon as your limbs improve

A big jump in draw weight usually means new arrows. As you improve your limbs, move to cheap aluminium-carbon arrows, spined for your new draw weight, rather than expensive shafts. It starts to matter when you shoot longer distances. The cheapest I know of is the Merlin Alimax. The next step up is the Pandarus CA320.

Be careful with shaft charts. They are quite inaccurate for these arrows. Before you buy, talk to other archers who shoot them at a similar draw weight and arrow length.

I don't recommend pure carbon arrows. Most archery clubs shoot on multi-purpose fields, and lost carbon arrows are notoriously hard to find, which can cause a lot of problems for the club. Many clubs don't allow them for this reason. Aluminium-carbon arrows have an aluminium core, so a metal detector can find them. Pure carbon arrows can't be found this way.

Sights and buttons

Keep the Shibuya DX pressure button from your starter kit: you don't need to upgrade it.

The Avalon Classic Carbon from the starter kit is a great sight, and you don't need to upgrade it. If you want something premium as a next step, I recommend the Shibuya Ultima RC3 or RC4.

Strings

Any 8125 string will do. The only brand I recommend is Reign: in my experience, Reign strings are the most consistent straight out of the box, and last the longest.

Risers, if you started on an entry riser

If you started on an entry riser, now is the time to upgrade. For the best quality for the price, I recommend CNC-machined risers from Mybo and WNS: see the riser table.

Looking further ahead

Don't rush this. Choose advanced kit yourself, once your draw weight and draw length have been stable for at least a season. By then, you'll know what you like better than any guide can tell you. Why I stop at intermediate →

A short look at what's out there for the next big step:

  • Risers: the current top risers from W&W Wiawis (Meta DX, Meta LX, ATF-DX and ATF-EX) and Hoyt (Xceed 2).
  • Limbs: advanced limbs, beyond the list above. Buy them only once your draw weight has been stable for at least a season.
  • Sights: the Shibuya Ultima RC3 or RC4.
  • Pressure buttons: the Beiter button.
  • Stabilisers: the current RamRods (Ultra 4 and Vektor 2) and Shrewd (Revel and RevX) ranges.
