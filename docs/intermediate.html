Intermediate kit

What to upgrade next, once you shoot regularly and enter club competitions. If you bought a good riser at the start, you keep it: most upgrades from here are small parts, plus new limbs and arrows as your draw weight goes up. Expect about £245–£300 for the upgrades below.

Who this is for

  • You shoot two or three times a week and enter club or county shoots.
  • You're working towards Archery GB classifications (Archer, Bowman and Master Bowman tiers).
  • Your draw weight is still going up, but more slowly than in your first year.
Where to spend: the same rule as for starter kit. Spend on the riser, sight, pressure button and stabilisers, which you keep. Save on limbs and arrows until your draw weight stops going up.

Arrows: stay on aluminium

I don't recommend carbon arrows. Most archery clubs shoot on multi-purpose fields, and lost carbon arrows are notoriously hard to find, which can cause a lot of problems for the club. Many clubs don't allow carbon arrows for this reason. Stay on aluminium arrows such as Easton Platinum Plus, and buy a new set when your draw weight changes the spine you need.

Limbs: cheap until your draw weight is stable

Keep buying budget limbs while your draw weight is still going up. Buy better limbs only once your draw weight has stayed the same for several months. Chiltern Archery runs a limb exchange scheme (£95–£125) for archers moving up in draw weight.
