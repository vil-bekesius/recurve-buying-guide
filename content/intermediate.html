Intermediate kit

What to upgrade next, once you shoot regularly and enter club competitions. If you bought a good riser at the start, you keep it: most upgrades from here are small parts, plus new limbs and arrows as your draw weight goes up.

Who this is for

  • You shoot two or three times a week and enter club or county shoots.
  • You're working towards Archery GB classifications (Archer, Bowman and Master Bowman tiers).
  • Your draw weight is still going up, but more slowly than in your first year.
Where to spend: the same rule as for starter kit. Spend on the riser, sight and pressure button, which you keep. Stabilisers can be good value. Save on limbs and arrows until your draw weight stops going up.

Arrows: cheap aluminium-carbon as your limbs improve

As you improve your limbs, move to cheap aluminium-carbon arrows, spined for your new draw weight. It starts to matter when you shoot longer distances. The cheapest I know of is the Merlin Alimax. The next step up is the Pandarus CA320.

Be careful with shaft charts. They are quite inaccurate for these arrows. Before you buy, talk to other archers who shoot them at a similar draw weight and arrow length.

I don't recommend pure carbon arrows. Most archery clubs shoot on multi-purpose fields, and lost carbon arrows are notoriously hard to find, which can cause a lot of problems for the club. Many clubs don't allow them for this reason.

Limbs: cheap until your draw weight is stable

Keep buying budget limbs while your draw weight is still going up. Buy better limbs only once your draw weight has stayed the same for at least six months. Some shops run limb exchange schemes for archers moving up in draw weight, so ask when you buy.

Stabilisers

Complete your set with an extender, a V-bar and two side rods that match your long rod.

Sights, buttons and clickers

I recommend the Beiter clicker. Keep the Shibuya DX pressure button from your starter kit: you don't need to upgrade it.

The Avalon Classic Carbon from the starter kit is a great sight, and you don't need to upgrade it. If you want something premium as a next step, I recommend the Shibuya Ultima RC3 or RC4.

Strings

Any 8125 string will do. The only brand I recommend is Reign: in my experience, Reign strings are the most consistent straight out of the box, and last the longest.

Risers, if you started on a budget riser

If you started on an entry riser, now is the time to upgrade. For the best quality for the price, I recommend CNC-machined risers from Mybo and WNS. A good intermediate riser will stay with you for many years.
