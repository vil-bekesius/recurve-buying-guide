Limbs, draw weight and arrows

Limbs and arrows depend on your draw weight, and most archers move up in draw weight in their first year or two. Buy these parts to suit where you are now, not where you hope to be.

Choosing limbs

  • Start low. For your first limbs, choose 22–26 lb at most: a weight you can shoot for 60 arrows with good form and control. Power is nothing without control.
  • Move up only when you're ready. Consider the next step in draw weight only when a full day of shooting leaves you with little or no fatigue.
  • Step up by about 2 lb. The best choice is new limbs that, fully wound out, are about 2 lb heavier than your old limbs fully wound in. This gives you room to grow without shocking your muscles with a big jump.
  • Don't overbow. Limbs that are much too heavy cause bad technique that is hard to fix. If you won't shoot regularly, buy the weight that is comfortable today.
  • Use the limb bolts. On most ILF risers the limb bolts change draw weight by roughly ±2–3 lb, which buys you more time from a set of limbs.
  • Bow length comes from draw length. The shop chooses 66", 68" or 70" from your draw length and riser length (25" or 27").
  • Stay ILF. ILF (International Limb Fitting) limbs fit almost any ILF riser, including Hoyt's Grand Prix fitting, so riser and limbs can be upgraded separately.
  • Cheap limbs first, top limbs later. Buy premium carbon limbs only once your draw weight has been stable for several months.

Choosing arrows

  • Always buy arrows in a shop. Staff measure your draw length and choose the spine from your draw weight, arrow length and point weight.
  • Let the shop decide the length. The shop will cut your arrows to suit your draw length, with some room for it to grow.
  • New draw weight, new spine. A big jump in draw weight usually means new arrows, so don't buy expensive shafts while you are still moving up.
