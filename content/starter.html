Starter kit

Your first bow of your own. I recommend choosing each part yourself: a good CNC-machined riser, with cheap limbs and arrows that you replace as your draw weight goes up.

Buy ILF, not a bolt-on training bow. ILF (International Limb Fitting) limbs fit almost any ILF riser, including Hoyt's Grand Prix fitting, so you can keep the riser and change limbs as your draw weight goes up. One-piece and bolt-on training bows can't be upgraded this way.

Who this is for

  • You've finished a beginners' course and shoot every week.
  • Your draw weight is still going up, so you want cheap limbs and arrows that you'll replace within a year.

Where to spend: on the riser, sight and pressure button. Save on limbs and arrows. Why spend there →

The riser

Buy an intermediate-level riser now, rather than an entry riser you'll replace in a year or two (buy once, cry once). For the best quality for the price, I recommend CNC-machined risers from Mybo and WNS. If your budget doesn't stretch that far, the Kinetic risers below are viable Olympic recurve alternatives. The Kinetic Vygo is a good wildcard if you haven't decided whether you'll shoot Olympic recurve or barebow: it suits both. The original Vygo is even cheaper.

First limbs: 22–26 lb at most

Think about the draw weight you can shoot for 60 arrows with good form and control, not the most you can pull. Power is nothing without control.

22–26 lb is the weight marked on the limbs, and it is for adults. Smaller or younger archers should start lower: ask the shop.

  • Don't overbow. Limbs that are much too heavy cause bad technique that is hard to fix. If you won't shoot regularly, buy the weight that is comfortable today.
  • Use the limb bolts. On most ILF risers the limb bolts change draw weight by roughly ±2–3 lb, which buys you more time from a set of limbs.
  • Bow length comes from draw length. The shop chooses 66", 68" or 70" from your draw length and riser length (25" or 27").

Arrows

  • Always buy arrows in a shop. Staff measure your draw length and choose the spine from your draw weight, arrow length and point weight.
  • Let the shop decide the length. The shop will cut your arrows to suit your draw length, with some room for it to grow.
  • Start on aluminium. Easton Platinum Plus arrows are cheap, tough and easy to replace when your spine changes.

Strings

Any 8125 string will do. The only brand I recommend is Reign: in my experience, Reign strings are the most consistent straight out of the box, and last the longest.

Stabilisers: start with a long rod

A long rod is enough to begin with, and Epic long rods are a good-value choice. Over time, you'll add an extender, a V-bar and two side rods. Choose a long rod from a range that also sells these parts, so they match when you complete the set.

Check the bow when you buy it

  • Look closely at the riser. Check both limb pockets and the riser body for anything that isn't symmetrical, such as one limb pocket sitting differently from the other.
  • Check the barebow weights. If the riser comes with barebow weights, make sure they fit as they should.
