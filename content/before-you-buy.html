Before you buy

Don't buy your first bow too early. The shop fits arrows to your draw length, so your draw has to be consistent before you go.

When you are ready

  1. Keep shooting club or beginner-course bows after your course until your form settles. Most archers need two to six months of regular shooting.
  2. Practise until your draw length is the same on every shot. Arrows cut too short are dangerous: an arrow drawn off the rest can hit your hand or bow.
  3. Ask a club coach which draw weights and bow length you should try in the shop.

Planning the shop visit

  1. Book an appointment. Most UK specialist shops fit customers by appointment, especially at weekends.
  2. Ring before you travel to check that the riser you want (model, length, colour) and limbs in your draw weight are in stock.
  3. Try several risers. A riser must feel right in your hand; grip shape matters more than brand.
  4. Allow about three hours. Measuring, building arrows and setting up a new bow takes time.
  5. Shoot the finished bow on the shop range if there is one, and ask the technician to show you the basic set-up (brace height, nocking point, centre shot).

Which grade is right for you?

GradeTypical archerRough complete cost
StarterFinished a beginners course, shooting weekly, first own bow£400–£540
Intermediate1–3 years in, club competitions, working towards Archery GB classifications£630–£1,210, plus parts you keep from your starter kit
AdvancedCounty, regional or national level; stable draw weight; wants kit that lasts many years£3,150–£3,700, bought in two stages
Rule of thumb: put your money into parts you don't grow out of: the riser, sight, pressure button and stabilisers. Limbs and arrows depend on draw weight, so they get replaced as you get stronger.

Shopping checklist

Print this page and take it to the shop.

  • Riser (handle), ILF fitting
  • Limbs (ILF)
  • String
  • Arrow rest
  • Pressure button (plunger)
  • Sight
  • Long rod stabiliser
  • Arrows, fitted in the shop
  • Finger tab
  • Armguard
  • Finger or wrist sling
  • Chest guard (optional)
  • Quiver and belt
  • Bow stringer
  • Bow stand
  • Case (rucksack or hard case)
  • Arrow puller
  • Bracing height gauge (bow square)
  • String wax
  • Allen keys, metric and imperial
